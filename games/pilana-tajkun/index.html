Mill books

What brings money in, and what takes it

SAWMILL
TYCOON

Logs ride the belt, the saw cuts them, forklifts load the trucks, and the trucks haul the wood across the river to the buyer. Invest what you earn and grow the whole business.

🖐️Drag to look around the map. Scroll or pinch to zoom. 👆Tap a machine — the saw, the veneer lathe, a forklift, a truck, the planer — and buy its upgrades from the panel. 🪚Buy more saws. Each new lane cuts logs, and they all fall into the same bin. Sacks between the belts sell the sawdust from those cuts. 📋The green card by the money is the next job. Finish it and the mill pays a bonus. Shop buyers also leave a tip once the stall learns how. 🌲The forest is not endless. A lumberjack takes several swings to fell a tree, then carries the log along the path to the deck at the belts. It counts only when he drops it there. If the stumpage fee is short he steps off the path and waits, then pays when the money is there. A faster haul gets the log to the deck sooner. Trees grow back slowly, and each one costs stumpage. Buy more forest when the deck starts to fall, or the saws run out. 🪵The planers sit on the east side of the bin, where the saw used to be. The saw belts are on the west side and still drop pieces into the bin. Each planer has its own worker, who picks wood up at the bin's east side and stacks the boards on the pallets just east of the machines. If a forklift blocks him he steps past after a moment. Forklifts share one dashed line between the bin and the trucks, and they park well back from the truck row so the concrete between them stays open. They drive through each other, so a pile-up does not freeze the lane. If they still sit still, open the forklift panel and tap Unstick. At the bin they use one gate: the rest wait in a line to the east, and the one inside backs onto that line before the next one drives in. Two more gates can be bought on the bin. Buy another pallet when you need room for more boards. ⚙️When the bin is overflowing, a chipper beside the saw chews the extra wood. Buy another tile to make the bin itself bigger. 🌀A veneer lathe sits just west of the saw belt. When the bin has wood to spare, a log spins, a knife peels a sheet, and the sheet winds into a roll. The roll sells for more than the cut wood would on a truck. A second and a third lathe can be bought when the bin can feed them. Each one takes a wage. 🏪The shop stands in the row with the joinery, the woodshed, and the kit shop. People come out of a tunnel and follow the path to the stall, one behind another. Other buyers drive around to the far side of that square, park in the marked bays, and walk back to a stall. The hauler, cart, or belt keeps filling the pallets whenever there is room, even when nobody is waiting. The logs stay stacked until the seller hands them over. If the stall has nothing coming, a long wait sends them back into the tunnel. The seller takes a wage. 🪑The furniture stand is beside the shop. The joiner walks to the log deck, or to the board pile when boards are spare, carries the wood back, and you can see the saw working on the bench. Buy the belt and the mill brings the wood to the bench instead. Then he carries the stool, bench, or table over. At full speed that ride is a belt too. People from the same tunnel take the other branch of the path and wait while he is on a trip. If nothing is being built, they give up and walk back. 🔥The woodshed in the shop row splits extra cut logs into firewood. You can see the axe working. A worker, then a cart, then the mill belt all the way to the shed carries the bundles to the rack out front. The rack has to sell before the next one is split. 📦The kit shop combines cut logs and boards into a crate, a shed kit, or a cabin kit. The fitter fetches both, unless the belt is bought, in which case the mill brings the parts to the bench. He walks the finished kit down the side of the shop to the stand. At full speed a belt carries it there. Each finished kit sells for more than the parts would on their own. 🚢When the mill is already busy, an export desk by the buyer is where the extra money goes. Each contract is a small bump in truck pay and costs a lot more than the last. 🏭Every so often the buyer offers double price on logs or on boards. Take it and a truck that pulls out before the timer ends sells that cargo for double. The yard keeps working. If a full fork of that cargo is ready, the forklifts load it first. 🏗️The highway turns in at the timber buyer, then a second turn runs south to the frame yard. The yard buys finished boards. It pays more per board than the log trucks, but only a few at a time, and it leaves some boards at the mill for furniture and rush orders. 🌲A dirt trail leaves the highway just before the buyer and runs south through the woods, ending at a tall pine. The ranger cabin makes trees grow back faster. The charcoal kiln burns spare cut wood into sacks, and only while the bin still has wood left. The nursery at the end plants saplings, and sells the extra ones once the woods are full. 🚐Rush vans use their own lot off the highway, down a short drive that meets the road at the edge. A blue forklift stays behind the shops, picks up wood, boards, firewood, or furniture there, and loads the van from the rear. Buy another bay when you want two or three loaded at once — extra vans wait beside the drive. The same lot also buys a bigger grab, a faster loader, quicker vans, more time on the clock, and better pay when an order is filled. 🚚Buy more trucks on the empty parking spots, including two more on the east end of the row, and bigger beds so each trip hauls more. An empty spot past those can be bought on its own. Diesel comes out of the sale when a truck dumps. The fuel yard just north of the parking holds the tanks — buy a bigger tank there and each load costs less. Coming back, a truck takes the first free spot. The whole crew takes a wage about once a minute, so a big crew with slow sales will spend the cash as fast as it comes in. 🚧There's a toll gate on the bridge. When trucks get stuck and angry, speed it up. 🛣️Widen the road. A wider road gives the trucks more room. The sign by the pad shows the new speed.
